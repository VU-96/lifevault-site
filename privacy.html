Privacy policy

LifeVault is built so that we never receive your information. This page explains exactly what that means, and the few places where someone else is involved.

Effective 18 October 2026

The short version

  • Everything you put into LifeVault stays on your phone, encrypted.
  • The app cannot connect to the internet. It has no accounts, analytics, advertising or crash reporting.
  • We do not collect, receive, sell or share your information, because we never have it.
  • A backup file or a shared PDF leaves the app only when you create it and choose where it goes.

1. Who we are

LifeVault is published by Valeed Labs, a one-person studio in India. In this policy, "we" and "us" mean Valeed Labs, and "the app" means the LifeVault app for Android.

You can reach us at valeedlabs@gmail.com.

2. Information in the app

You may store names of people in your household and details of their documents: document type, title, number, issuing country, issue and expiry dates, notes, and scans or photos of the documents. Some of this can be sensitive, such as identity numbers or a medical card.

All of it is stored only on your phone, in an encrypted database and encrypted files. None of it is sent to us or to anyone else. The app does not ask Android for internet access, so it has no means of sending it.

We cannot see, read, copy, restore or delete what you store. If you ask us for a copy of your information, we have nothing to give you, because it exists only on your phone and in any backups you made yourself.

3. What the app does not do

  • No account, sign-in, email address or phone number.
  • No analytics, usage tracking or advertising identifiers.
  • No advertising.
  • No crash reporting sent from the app.
  • No cloud sync. Android's own cloud backup and phone-to-phone transfer are switched off for LifeVault, so your vault is not copied to a Google account.

Text recognition. To read the code lines on passports and ID cards, LifeVault uses Google's ML Kit text recognition (ML Kit terms), with its model built into the app, so the reading happens on your phone. Google states that ML Kit does not send the images or the text it reads to Google. It can try to contact Google for updates and to send statistics about how the feature performs and how much it is used. LifeVault has no internet permission, so none of that can leave your phone; we checked on a phone that the app sends no network traffic.

4. Permissions the app uses

Text in a scanned document, such as the machine-readable lines of a passport, is read on your phone by software built into the app. The picture is not sent anywhere to be read.

5. When information leaves the app

Only when you make it do so:

  • Backup. You can export one backup file, encrypted with a password you choose. You decide where to save it. If you put it in a cloud storage service or send it to someone, that service's or person's handling of the file is outside our control.
  • Sharing a document. You can create a PDF of a document and pass it to another app, such as email or a messaging app. The PDF is made on your phone and is not encrypted. What happens to it after you share it depends on the app and the person you send it to.
  • Copying. If you copy a document number, it passes through Android's clipboard, which other apps on your phone may be able to read for a short time.

6. Other people's information

You can store documents that belong to family members. You are responsible for having their agreement, or the right to hold their documents, for example as a parent or guardian. We never receive this information either.

7. Children

LifeVault is a tool for adults managing household paperwork and is not directed at children. A parent or guardian may store a child's documents. Because the app collects nothing, we do not collect information from children or from anyone else.

8. Keeping and deleting your information

Your information stays on your phone until you remove it. You can delete a single document or person, or use Settings, then "Erase all data", to wipe the vault. Uninstalling the app also removes it. Backup files and PDFs you saved or shared elsewhere are not affected; delete those where you put them.

9. Your rights

Privacy laws in many countries give you rights over personal information that an organisation holds about you, such as the right to see it, correct it or have it deleted. For the information in the app, you already have complete control on your own phone, and we hold none of it. If you contact us by email, you have those rights over the email itself; write to us at valeedlabs@gmail.com and we will act on your request.

You also have the right to complain to the data protection authority where you live.

10. Google Play

You download LifeVault from Google Play. Google handles that download, and its own privacy policy applies to it. Google gives app developers summary figures such as the number of installs and ratings and, where a phone's owner has agreed to share diagnostics with Google, technical crash information. We add no tracking of our own.

11. This website

This website sets no cookies, runs no analytics and loads nothing from other companies' servers. Like any website, it is delivered by a hosting provider, whose servers may keep a short technical log of requests, including the visitor's internet address. This site is hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub logs visitors' internet addresses for security purposes, as its own privacy statement describes.

12. If you email us

We receive your email address and whatever you write. We use it only to answer you. Emails stay in that inbox until you ask us to delete yours, which we will. Please do not send us document numbers or copies of your documents.

13. Changes to this policy

If the app or this website changes in a way that affects your privacy, we will update this page and its date before the change takes effect. The current version is always at this address.
