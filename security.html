How LifeVault protects your data

What is encrypted, where the keys live, and what the app cannot protect you from.

Nothing leaves the phone

LifeVault does not request Android's internet permission, or permission to see the state of your network. Android does not let an app open a network connection without that permission. There is no server on our side, no account, and no code in the app for analytics, advertising or crash reporting.

Android's cloud backup and phone-to-phone transfer are switched off for the app, so the vault is not copied to a Google account behind your back.

Encrypted on your phone

  • Database. Your people and documents are kept in a SQLCipher database. It is encrypted with a random 256-bit key created on your phone the first time you open the app.
  • The key. That key is held in secure storage protected by the Android Keystore. It never leaves the phone.
  • Scans and photos. Each attachment and its thumbnail is encrypted as its own file with AES-GCM. When you view one, it is decrypted in memory, not written back to storage in readable form.

The lock

  • You choose the lock: a six-digit PIN just for LifeVault, your phone's own screen lock (Android 11 and newer), or none.
  • The PIN: the app stores a hash of it made with Argon2id, not the PIN itself. Wrong attempts are slowed down: after repeated mistakes you must wait before trying again, from 30 seconds up to an hour, and the wait survives restarting the app.
  • The phone's screen lock: Android's own prompt asks for the phone's PIN, pattern, password or fingerprint, and Android counts wrong attempts. The app is told only whether the check passed.
  • With the PIN you can add fingerprint or face unlock. Only Android's strongest class of biometrics is accepted, and the unlock is tied to a key in the phone's secure hardware: if a fingerprint or face is added to the phone, the key is destroyed and only the PIN opens the vault until you turn biometrics on again.
  • The app locks when you leave it: immediately, or after 1 or 5 minutes, as you choose.
  • Screenshots and the preview in the recent-apps list are blocked unless you turn that off.
  • Other apps cannot draw over LifeVault while it is open (Android 12 and newer), so nothing can sit on top of the PIN pad.

Reminders that give nothing away

A reminder says, for example, "A document expires in 7 days." It never includes a document number, a title or a person's name, and its content is hidden on a locked screen where Android supports that. Tapping it opens the app, which asks for your PIN first.

Backups

A backup is one file containing your documents and attachments. It is encrypted with AES-GCM using a key derived from a password you choose, with Argon2id, which is designed to make guessing slow. The file is only as strong as that password. Choose a long one, and do not reuse it.

Copying and sharing

When you copy a document number, the app marks it as sensitive, so Android shows dots instead of the number in its clipboard preview. After 60 seconds the app clears it from the clipboard, provided it is still the last thing you copied. If you are in another app at that moment, it is cleared when you come back to LifeVault, because Android does not let an app look at the clipboard while it is in the background. A PDF you share is made on your phone and is not encrypted, so send it only to people and apps you trust.

What it cannot protect you from

  • Someone who knows your PIN, or, if you lock with the phone's screen lock, your phone's PIN, pattern or password; or someone whose fingerprint or face is registered on your phone.
  • A phone that has been rooted or infected with malicious software that can read the screen or memory while the vault is open.
  • A weak backup password, or a backup file and its password kept together.
  • A PDF or backup after it has left the app.
  • Forgetting your PIN. There is no recovery; see Support.

Report a security problem

If you find a weakness, please tell us at valeedlabs@gmail.com before making it public, so it can be fixed.
